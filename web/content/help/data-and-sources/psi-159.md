PSI-159 ACME CFI-G is a NASA microgravity investigation in the PSI (Physical Sciences Informatics) archive: “Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments / Cool Flames Investigation with Gases”. Release 20-Nov-2025, version 1. DOI [10.60555/cyt1-kh64](https://doi.org/10.60555/cyt1-kh64). License: CC0-1.0.

In plain words: this page says what PSI-159 looked at, what conditions PYRION could read from it (with the place in NASA's files where each one is written), and what it could not read. 2188 of its 2188 records are usable as evidence.

## What it studied
PSI objective (quoted, section `objective`):

> 1. Plan and support flight testing on the International Space Station (ISS). 2. Determine the conditions for which cool diffusion flames occur for propane, n-butane, and n-pentane; normal and inverse flames; and a broad range of Zst, Tad, and fluent flow rate. 3. Support the flight tests with advanced CFD simulations. 4. Identify or develop a chemical kinetics mechanism that yields good agreement between the CFD predictions and the ISS measurements. 5. Disseminate the results as widely as possible.

PSI hypothesis (quoted, section `hypothesis`):

> The hypotheses of this research are that testing aboard the ISS can demonstrate the following for the first time, contributing to an improved understanding of cool flame kinetics. 1. Steady-state cool diffusion flames on spherical porous burners. 2. Cool diffusion flames burning propane, n-butane, and n-pentane. 3. Cool diffusion flames for a wide range of Zst, Tad, and pressures. 4. Inverse cool diffusion flames. 5. Cool diffusion flames for which a decrease in fluent flow rate will cause extinction, whereas an increase will cause a transition to a hot diffusion flame, with oscillation possible between hot and cool flames.

As recorded by PSI: flight program: International Space Station (ISS); hardware: ACME, Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR); managing center: Glenn Research Center (GRC).
