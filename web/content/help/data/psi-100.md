PSI-100 SAFFIRE-III is a NASA microgravity investigation in the PSI (Physical Sciences Informatics) archive: “Spacecraft Fire Experiment-III”. Release 18-Sep-2024, version 4. DOI [10.60555/040q-4h36](https://doi.org/10.60555/040q-4h36). License: CC0-1.0.

In plain words: this topic explains PSI-100, its cited sources and extraction limits. The [investigation sheet](/investigations/PSI-100) and [Data page](/data) provide the current records, statuses and coverage.

## What it studied
PSI objective (quoted, section `objective`):

> The objectives of this investigation were to: (1) determine how rapidly a large scale fire grows in low-gravity and (2) investigate the low-g flammability limits compared to those obtained in NASA’s normal gravity material flammability screening test.

PSI hypothesis (quoted, section `hypothesis`):

> This investigation hypothesizes that due to large differences between fire behavior in normal and reduced gravity, the use of terrestrial fires and fire standards to design spacecraft fire safety systems presents an inherent risk to the vehicle because of the significant level of uncertainty. While this approach has been successful thus far, the uncertainty and risk will increase as exploration missions venture further from earth with considerably longer transit times for a safe return. There is a significant lack of microgravity data available for spacecraft designers on which to base their fire safety designs and procedures. [...]

As recorded by PSI: flight program: International Space Station (ISS); hardware: Saffire III enclosure in Cygnus; managing center: Glenn Research Center (GRC).

## Platform and gravity, as stated
- Platform: **Cygnus** (quote: “Saffire III enclosure in Cygnus”, `section=experimentHardware`).
- Platform: **Cygnus resupply vehicle** (quote: “The Saffire-III experiments were performed on the Orbital Science’s Cygnus resupply vehicle after it deberthed from the ISS.”, `section=approach`).
- Gravity regime: **microgravity** for test(s) `*` (quote: “Flame spread over a large thin charring surface in low-gravity showed that steady flame spread was possible (unlike normal gravity)”, `page=18`, 20180005168.pdf).

## What could not be extracted, and why
Records kept as `insufficient_evidence` carry their reason in the `note` field. The most common ones:

- `sample_thickness`: cm x 10
- `gravity_regime`: Objective statement, not a statement of the test condition. Source term 'low-gravity'; no g-level stated; mapped to the controlled term microgravity. [...]
- `gravity_regime`: Objective statement covering SAFFIRE-I and SAFFIRE-III together, not a statement of the test condition. Source term 'low-gravity'; no g-level stated; mapped to the controlled term microgravity. [...]
- `gravity_regime`: Operational-requirements document specific to SAFFIRE-III, but the sentence covers Saffire-I and -III together and describes what the sample is meant to demonstrate, not a measured test condition. [...]
- `gravity_regime`: Research-impact summary, not a statement of the test condition; no g-level stated. In the PSI-100 record this section opens with 'The Saffire-I experiment provides a new way to study a realistic fire on an exploration vehicle', [...]

Ground (1 g) data check: `none` in the ground-test inventory (see the article on the ground inventory).

Publications are listed on the [investigation sheet](/investigations/PSI-100) with their identifiers and sources.
