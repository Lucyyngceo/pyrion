PSI-106 SLICE is a NASA microgravity investigation in the PSI (Physical Sciences Informatics) archive: “Structure and Liftoff In Combustion Experiment”. Release 18-Sep-2024, version 3. DOI [10.60555/pm12-rg61](https://doi.org/10.60555/pm12-rg61). License: CC0-1.0.

In plain words: this topic explains PSI-106, its cited sources and extraction limits. The [investigation sheet](/investigations/PSI-106) and [Data page](/data) provide the current records, statuses and coverage.

## What it studied
PSI objective (quoted, section `objective`):

> Objective: The objective of this investigation is to better understand the structure and stability of laminar diffusion flames in microgravity. Specific objectives of this investigation were: 1. To investigate the structure of laminar diffusion flames in microgravity to understand the stabilizing mechanisms at the flames base. 2. Determine the liftoff velocity limits for different fuels and burner diameters. 3. Evaluate the impact of fuel dilution with nitrogen on soot formation and flame stability. 4. Quantify the effects of microgravity on flame length and radiative heat loss compared to normal gravity conditions. 5. [...]

PSI hypothesis (quoted, section `hypothesis`):

> The hypothesis is that the structure and stability of laminar diffusion flames in microgravity are significantly influenced by burner diameter, coflow velocity, and fuel composition and are fundamentally different than flames observed on Earth.

As recorded by PSI: flight program: International Space Station (ISS); managing center: Glenn Research Center (GRC).

## Platform and gravity, as stated
- Platform: **ISS MSG** (quote: “The SPICE hardware is within the ISS MSG in the image to the right. While the SPICE investigation has been specifically focused on a study of soot production and oxidation within flames, the hardware can be used without [...]”, `page=2`).
- Platform: **International Space Station (ISS)** (quote: “International Space Station (ISS)”, `section=flightProgram`).
- **No usable gravity-regime record.** Gravity is only recorded when a source sentence states it for the tests; the platform is never used for it. The engine reads gravity here as insufficient evidence.

## Fuel and flame configuration
fuel phase: gas; diluent: N2; flow configuration: coflow; fuel descriptions: 100% CH4, 20% C2H4 (balance N2), 40% CH4 (balance N2) including 2 pyrometry calibration tests and 2 fan calibrations, 70% CH4 (balance N2) including 1 pyrometry calibration test, 40% CH4 (balance N2), 70% CH4 (balance N2) where the fuel bottle was not emptied (in contrast to most days), 100% C2H6, 70% CH4 (balance N2) including 1 pyrometry calibration test and 2 fan calibrations.

## What could not be extracted, and why
Records kept as `insufficient_evidence` carry their reason in the `note` field. The most common ones:

- `fuel`: the date cell says this day was SPICE testing
- `gravity_regime`: Objective statement, not a sentence stating the gravity condition of the tests. Same section, item 4: 'Quantify the effects of microgravity on flame length and radiative heat loss compared to normal gravity conditions.'; [...]
- `gravity_regime`: Purpose statement in a NASA Glenn fact sheet outside PSI. The fact sheet describes SLICE in the future tense (e.g. page 2: 'SLICE will include testing where the fuel flow is fixed [...]'), i.e. as planned, not as run. [...]
- `gravity_regime`: Program-level sentence in the NASA ISS Researcher's Guide, page 45. 'Microgravity' qualifies the research program and 'in the MSG facility' is a platform; neither states the gravity level of the SLICE tests. [...]

Ground (1 g) data check: `none` in the ground-test inventory (see the article on the ground inventory).
