PSI-107 SPICE is a NASA microgravity investigation in the PSI (Physical Sciences Informatics) archive: “Smoke Point In Co-flow Experiment”. Release 18-Sep-2024, version 4. DOI [10.60555/9kbh-e962](https://doi.org/10.60555/9kbh-e962). License: CC0-1.0.

In plain words: this topic explains PSI-107, its cited sources and extraction limits. The [investigation sheet](/investigations/PSI-107) and [Data page](/data) provide the current records, statuses and coverage.

## What it studied
PSI objective (quoted, section `objective`):

> The objective of SPICE is to better understand the complex process of combustion by observing flames in a microgravity environment aboard the International Space Station (ISS) and analogous ground experiments. Specific objectives were: (1) Identify the laminar smoke-point properties of non-buoyant jet diffusion flames under various co-flow conditions in microgravity. (2) Determine the effects of burner diameter and co-flow velocity on the luminous flame lengths and smoke points. (3) Quantify the sooting propensity of different fuels, such as ethylene, propane, and propylene mixtures. [...]

PSI hypothesis (quoted, section `hypothesis`):

> The research hypothesizes that by minimizing gravity-driven transport phenomena scientists can study the laminar smoke-point properties of non-buoyant jet diffusion flames. Flame properties are expected to differ significantly from those observed under normal gravity.

As recorded by PSI: flight program: International Space Station (ISS); hardware: Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG); managing center: Glenn Research Center (GRC).

## Platform and gravity, as stated
- Platform: **International Space Station (ISS)** (quote: “International Space Station (ISS)”, `section=flightProgram`).
- Platform: **Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG)** (quote: “Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG)”, `section=experimentHardware`).
- Gravity regime: **microgravity** for test(s) `*` (quote: “SPICE is the first experiment that studies soot processes of laminar jet diffusion flame under long term microgravity condition with air coflow.”, `page=16`, PSI-107_Reports_SPICE_FinalReport.pdf).
- Gravity regime: **microgravity** for test(s) `*` (quote: “Smoke points of 55 microgravity gas-jet diffusion flames in coflowing air were measured in earth orbit.”, `page=24`, PSI-107_Reports_SPICE_FinalReport.pdf).

## Fuel and flame configuration
fuel phase: gas; diluent: N2; flow configuration: coflow; fuel descriptions: Propylene, 75 % Propylene, 50 % Propylene, Propane, Ethylene, 75% Propylene, Methane.

## What could not be extracted, and why
Records kept as `insufficient_evidence` carry their reason in the `note` field. The most common ones:

- `flow_velocity`: negative air rate: not a possible co-flow speed as written
- `o2_mole_fraction`: Section 'SPICE FLIGHT TESTS' (printed page 13). This is the band within which ISS cabin air is kept, not a per-test O2 measurement. No O2 measurement inside the MSG is reported.
- `o2_mole_fraction`: confidence ambiguous (Footnote a of Table 1. The words left out are ', uf,'. The PDF text layer splits the subscripted u_f across lines. [...]
- `pressure`: Same footnote as R10. 101 kPa is the pressure ASSUMED for the velocity and Reynolds-number calculations. No sentence in the sources read states the actual MSG or cabin pressure during the tests (see not_found).

Ground (1 g) data check: `yes_table` in the ground-test inventory (see the article on the ground inventory).
