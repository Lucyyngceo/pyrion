## What it studied
PSI objective (quoted, section `objective`):

> The focus of DAFT / DAFT-2 was to mitigate the risk for the Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment (SAME). SAME relied on a commercially available condensate nuclei counter, the TSI P-Trak, to quantify smoke aerosols in a microgravity environment, however there were concerns that th P-Trak would not be reliable in microgravity as it relies on gravity to recycle isopropyl alcohol used in the measurement process. DAFT / DAFT-2 tests that a modified P-Trak particulate counter would be accurate in microgravity. [...]

PSI hypothesis (quoted, section `hypothesis`):

> The TSI P-Trak operates by condensing isopropanol around aerosol particulates that are subsequently laser counted. It was hypothesized that the P-Trak would not work in microgravity due to two potential failure modes that exist. 1) the system would clog or become obscured by isopropanol, and 2) the internal air-flow dynamics of the optics chamber of the device will cause an over counting of particulates. The TSI P-Trak was modified for microgravity use via using capillary action to recycle the isopropanol. Therefore, there are two basic hypotheses: 1) that the P-Trak, as modified for use in SAME, would function normally in microgravity. [...]

As recorded by PSI: flight program: International Space Station (ISS); hardware: TSI P-Trak; managing center: Glenn Research Center (GRC).
